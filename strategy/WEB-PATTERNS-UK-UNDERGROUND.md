# WEB PATTERNS — UNDERGROUND STREETWEAR SITES

Companion to `UK-UNDERGROUND-PLAYBOOK.md`, which covers brand and community
mechanics. **This document is about the websites specifically** — structure,
entry, and how commerce sits inside the world.

**Method and its limit, stated up front.** The four sites named
(phrasestudios.com, carsicko.com, clints.co, placesplusfaces.com) are blocked
by this environment's network egress, so I could not load and inspect them
directly. What follows is built from documented accounts of how these brands
and their sites operate, and it is therefore about **structural patterns**, not
a visual audit. **Before build, someone should open all four and record what
they actually do** — see §6.

**CLAUDE.md rule 4 governs everything below.** We take mechanics. We never take
logos, type, slogans, graphics, layouts, campaigns, colours or identity.

---

## 1. PLACES+FACES — the archive that became a brand

**What they did.** Founded 2013 by Imran Ciesay and Solomon Boyede. It began as
**a place to dump an archive** of backstage photographs — Ciesay shooting in
New York, Soulz in London — posted to a Tumblr. It grew into zines, a print
magazine (2017), club nights, mixes with UK artists, and then product. Accounts
consistently note they were **not observing the scene but integrated into it**:
subjects photographed in their own environment, not styled shoots.

**Why it works.** The archive came first and the product came second, so the
product inherited a reason to exist. The brand is trusted because the
photographs were being taken before there was anything to sell.

**What MAJDAL takes — and this is the most important finding in the document:**

> **Places+Faces is the precedent for the Archive Desk homepage.** The strongest
> validation available that an archive-first brand is not a theory. It is how
> the most culturally credible brand in this set was actually built.

It also confirms the *posture* MAJDAL has already committed to: **participant,
not observer.** Their credibility came from being inside the scene. MAJDAL's
equivalent is not proximity to a scene — it is custody of a documented lineage,
which is why Gate 2 review and paid Palestinian collaboration are not optional
extras. They are what makes us a participant rather than an extractor.

**Never copy:** their photography style, name structure, logo, magazine format,
event format.

## 2. CORTEIZ — the gate, and why it really exists

**What they do.** Password-locked site; clues distributed on social; access to
drops reserved for those paying attention; unpredictable launches, severely
limited stock.

**The part usually missed.** Coverage of the locked site repeatedly appears
alongside warnings about **counterfeit sites selling knock-offs**. The gate is
not only mystique — it is **address control**. When a brand's audience learns
there is exactly one real way in, the fakes lose their route.

**What MAJDAL takes.** Already specified as THE PASSWORD IS A FACT
(`UK-UNDERGROUND-PLAYBOOK.md` §4.1). This finding adds a second, practical
reason to run it, and a duty: **the site must state plainly what the one real
address is**, because a brand handling Palestinian material being impersonated
is a cultural risk, not just a revenue one.

**Never copy:** the bolo/map logo, "Rule The World", the £0.01 stunt, the
alley-cat drop format, the password-gate *as a shop mechanic*.

## 3. CARSICKO — absence as a mechanic

**What they did.** Founded February 2021 by Owen Otasowie, a former Premier
League footballer, during injury recovery. Grew without large ad spend —
"the clothes were real", worn by grime artists, footballers and creators, found
through social media and through each other. **In August 2024 the brand went
quiet. In May 2025 thousands turned up to a London pop-up** and it returned
with a new collection to an audience that had waited.

**Why it works.** Going quiet is only survivable if the community is real. When
it is, absence compounds anticipation — and the return is an event rather than
a post.

**What MAJDAL takes.** Permission to be quiet. MAJDAL's chapter cadence already
implies long gaps, and `CONTENT-SYSTEM.md` caps sales posts at 1 in 4. This is
evidence that **a gap is not a failure of marketing**. It also sets the real
test: a brand that can disappear and return to a full room has a community; one
that must post weekly to stay alive has an audience.

**Never copy:** the heart logo, tracksuit identity, graphics, footballer-founder
narrative.

## 4. CLINTS — hyperlocal, founder-made

**What they do.** Manchester label founded 2020 by Junior Clint, a former model
turned self-taught designer who learned through a sewing machine and YouTube.
Footwear-led, then apparel. Identity is explicitly and unmistakably Mancunian.

**Why it works.** Specific beats broad. A brand rooted in one named place is
harder to imitate than one rooted in a mood, and the founder's hand is visible
in the product.

**What MAJDAL takes.** Confirmation of the anchoring rule already in the
playbook: every chapter is tied to a real named place. MAJDAL's version is
stronger than a city, because al-Majdal is not a backdrop — it is the reason
the brand makes cloth at all.

**Never copy:** the identity, footwear silhouettes, logo, visual language.

## 5. PHRASE STUDIOS — a collision, flagged honestly

**Two corrections first.** Phrase Studios is **Belgian, not UK** — an
independent studio in **Ghent, founded 2023**, working under "From friends, to
friends", describing its lane as *Lowlands streetwear*.

**Now the part that matters.**

> **Phrase Studios already releases numbered chapters.** Documented as: small
> runs of **heavyweight cotton**, **not restocked** once sold through, each
> chapter **written around a single idea — a phrase, a place, a feeling**.

That is structurally very close to what MAJDAL has specified: chapters not
seasons, heavyweight cotton, no restock of a closed chapter, one subject per
chapter. **This was arrived at independently, but that does not matter to
anyone looking at both brands.**

**Assessment — not a reason to abandon the chapter system.** Chaptered,
non-restocked drops are not ownable, and plenty of brands run them. But the
rule in `BRAND-BOOK.md` §9 is that MAJDAL must be *difficult to imitate and
not read as an imitation*. So the differentiation has to be deliberate:

| | Phrase Studios | MAJDAL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter basis | "a phrase, a place, a feeling" | A **documented object** with sources — cloth, cactus, seed |
| What a chapter carries | Garments | Garments **plus** an archive, tiered evidence, a review gate, an impact accrual |
| Why no restock | Small-batch production | A chapter is a **closed subject**; core garments may continue under a permanent line, because pretending a good hoodie is unrepeatable is fake scarcity |
| Verification | — | Nothing ships without Gate 2 |

**Concrete language changes required.** Stop describing chapters in terms that
echo theirs. Specifically:
- Lead with **"a chapter is a subject, documented"**, never "a chapter is an
  idea / a feeling".
- Never pair "heavyweight cotton" with "numbered chapters" as the *headline*
  proposition — that combination is their lane. Our headline is the record.
- Keep "480 pieces" tied to the real purchase order, which is a factual claim
  they do not make.

**Also worth noting:** Phrase's site runs a large editorial **journal** (guides
to streetwear brands) alongside the store. That is a content-marketing engine,
and it is a legitimate pattern — but it is *not* what MAJDAL's archive is. Ours
is primary research about one town. Theirs is category commentary. **Do not let
the archive drift into listicles**; the moment it does, it becomes SEO content
and stops being the thing that makes the brand hard to copy.

**Never copy:** the name, the "From friends, to friends" line, their chapter
numbering presentation, journal format, product photography.

## 6. Cross-cutting patterns worth taking

| Pattern | Seen in | MAJDAL's version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Archive precedes product** | Places+Faces | Already the plan. The strongest validation in this set. |
| **One controlled address** | Corteiz | State the real URL plainly; make counterfeits obviously wrong |
| **Absence is allowed** | Carsicko | Chapter gaps are intended; no weekly posting to stay alive |
| **Named place, named founder** | Clints | al-Majdal; the founder's hand visible without a personality cult |
| **Small runs, real numbers** | Phrase, Carsicko | 480 = the actual PO. Never invented |
| **Sold out stays sold out** | Phrase, Carsicko | Closed chapters do not return; permanent line is named separately |

## 7. What this changes in the build

**Nothing structural — it sharpens the existing recommendation.**

1. **Concept 2 (THE ARCHIVE DESK) is confirmed**, and now has a precedent:
   Places+Faces built exactly this sequence — archive, then audience, then
   product. See `docs/02-CONCEPTS-HOMEPAGE.md`.
2. **Add an address-integrity line** to the site footer and the join flow: the
   one real URL, and a note that MAJDAL will never ask for payment elsewhere.
3. **Rewrite chapter-facing copy** per §5 so MAJDAL does not read as Phrase
   Studios in a different palette. This is a copy task, not a design task, and
   it should happen before any chapter page is finalised.
4. **Do not add a journal.** The temptation after seeing Phrase's traffic
   engine will be real. The archive is research, not commentary.

## 8. Before build — actually look at the sites

This document is structural, drawn from documented accounts, because the
domains are unreachable from this environment. Someone with a browser should
open all four and record: entry sequence, navigation labels and order, how a
product page is structured, how drops are announced, what the site does when
nothing is dropping, mobile navigation, and load time on a phone.

**The last two are the ones most people forget, and they are where most of
these sites are actually weakest** — which is where MAJDAL can be better rather
than merely different.
