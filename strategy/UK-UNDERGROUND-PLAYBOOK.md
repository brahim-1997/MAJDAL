# UK UNDERGROUND PLAYBOOK
## What actually makes these brands work, and MAJDAL's own version of it

**Read `brand/BRAND-BOOK.md` §9 rule 2 first.** We study these brands to
extract *mechanics*. We never reproduce their logos, type, slogans, graphics,
campaign concepts or distinctive identity. Every row below ends with a MAJDAL
implementation derived from **our own material** — cloth, cactus, seed, 48.

---

## 1. The engine underneath all of them

Strip the aesthetics and the successful UK underground brands run one engine:

> **Make people *do something* to belong. Then let them distribute it.**

Corteiz's password-gated site is the clearest example. Analysts describe it
precisely: a password gate *turns browsing into work, and work creates
commitment* — people who spend time getting in have already paid a small cost
before they see a product page. Clues get traded between fans, so distribution
moves into the community's hands rather than being bought.

That is the transferable part. Three components:

1. **Earned access** — you did something to get in.
2. **Physical activation** — something happened in real life, in a real place.
3. **Community distribution** — members carry the information, not ads.

## 2. The thing MAJDAL must not do

Here is the strategic judgement that shapes everything below, and it is the
most important paragraph in this document.

**Hype mechanics plus Palestinian history equals exploitation.** A
password-gated sixty-second scramble over a garment about erased villages would
be grotesque, and everyone would be right to say so. The brief already bans
fake scarcity; this is the same rule with higher stakes.

So MAJDAL keeps the engine and changes the act. Corteiz makes you chase a
location. **MAJDAL makes you carry a record forward.** Same mechanism — earned
access, physical activation, community distribution — completely different
thing being earned, and it is coherent with our subject instead of borrowed
against it.

If an activation would work equally well for a brand with no cultural subject,
it is a hype tactic and we do not run it.

## 4. MAJDAL activations — the three originals

These are ours. They derive from our subject and would make no sense for any
other brand, which is the test.

### 4.1 THE PASSWORD IS A FACT
*Earned access, our version.*

Each chapter window opens behind a single question answerable only from the
archive. Chapter 001: **how many metres made one dress?** (Eight.) Chapter 002:
**what plant still marks the villages?** The answer circulates through THE
ROOTS — members teach each other, which is exactly the distribution behaviour
we want, and what gets transmitted is the history.

**Why it beats a password:** a random string rewards being online at the right
moment. A fact rewards having read. The people who get in are the people who
engaged, and every person who asks "what's the answer?" gets taught something
true about al-Majdal.

**Anti-exclusion guard:** the question is never a trick, never timed to
seconds, and the archive is public and free. The gate is on the *window*, never
on the knowledge.

### 4.2 THE EXCHANGE
*Physical activation with real value transfer.*

Bring a garment you no longer wear to a MAJDAL activation. It is cleaned and
routed to a verified recipient supporting displaced people; you receive chapter
access. Every exchange is logged — count, destination, recipient, date — in the
impact ledger alongside the 15%.

**Why it is not a stunt:** the ledger makes it auditable, and the validator
will not let us report a destination we have not verified. If we cannot verify
a recipient, the activation does not run.

**Honest constraint:** used-clothing donation is frequently *unhelpful* to
recipient organisations and can undercut local markets. Before this runs, a
recipient must confirm in writing that they want garments rather than cash. If
they want cash, **we run the same activation and send money instead.** This is
exactly the check that separates a real programme from a photo opportunity.

### 4.3 THE SEED
*Fecundity, made participatory.*

With Chapter 003 — BALADI, each order carries baladi seed, and members plant it
and photograph what grows a season later. The community becomes the "next
generation carries it forward" line, literally, and the UGC is a plant rather
than a mirror selfie.

**Hard constraint, flagged now rather than discovered late:** seed is heavily
restricted across borders under phytosanitary and customs law, and posting it
internationally is often illegal. Options: (a) run it only in jurisdictions
where it is lawful; (b) ship a **seed certificate** redeemable from a local
supplier per region; (c) no physical seed at all — the activation becomes a
planting day at THE TABLE. Decide before the chapter is designed.
**Any association with the Palestine Heirloom Seed Library requires their prior
written agreement and payment.** We do not borrow another project's name.

## 5. What we take, what we refuse

**Take:** earned access · physical activation · community distribution ·
encoded marks · hyperlocal anchoring · craft transparency · product quality as
message · consistent graphic system · real artist relationships.

**Refuse:**
- **Fake scarcity.** Run sizes are real PO quantities.
- **Bot-war drops.** A sixty-second scramble is not community, it is a raffle.
- **Resale-driven hype.** We would rather sell out slowly to people who wear it.
- **Mystery with no answer.** 48 always resolves to one plain sentence.
- **Borrowed edge.** UK underground credibility comes from those founders'
  actual lives. Ours comes from al-Majdal. Importing their posture would be the
  same borrowing we refuse everywhere else.
- **Suffering imagery.** Never, under any mechanic.

## 6. Sequencing

The playbook does **not** start at week 1. `CHAPTER-001-LAUNCH-SEQUENCE.md`
front-loads research and community for eight weeks before anything is sold, and
that ordering stands.

| Chapter | Activation introduced |
| --- | --- |
| **001 — ROOTS** | THE PASSWORD IS A FACT (week 10 window) · THE TABLE, city one |
| **002 — SABR** | THE EXCHANGE, once a recipient has confirmed in writing |
| **003 — BALADI** | THE SEED, once the legal route is settled |

One new mechanic per chapter. A brand that runs every tactic at once looks like
it is performing being underground, which is the opposite of being it.
