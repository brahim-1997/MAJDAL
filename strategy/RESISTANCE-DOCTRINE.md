# RESISTANCE DOCTRINE

How MAJDAL holds Palestinian resistance as its subject without becoming a
slogan brand. This document exists because the brief contains a real tension
and it is better resolved deliberately than improvised under pressure.

## 1. The tension, stated plainly

- The brand's subject **is** Palestinian resistance and continuity.
- The brand **must not** become "constant political slogans" or generic
  Palestine merchandise.

Both are correct. Most brands resolve this by going vague — "inspired by
resilience" — which is cowardice, and it fails the decision framework's
authenticity test. MAJDAL resolves it the other way: **by being more specific,
not less political.**

## 2. The doctrine

> **Resistance, for MAJDAL, is continuity. We show what survived and who
> carried it — never a slogan, never a symbol without a source.**

This is not a softening. *Sumud* — the documented Palestinian concept of
steadfastness, of remaining present on the land — is resistance, and it is the
tradition MAJDAL sits inside. The weavers of al-Majdal who kept weaving after
expulsion were practising it.

**Three consequences:**

1. **Nouns, not adjectives.** A cloth. A cactus. A seed. A key. Each is a
   documented object with a verifiable history. "Resilience", "struggle" and
   "freedom" are adjectives wearing a coat, and they could belong to anyone.
2. **The verb is *carry*.** They carried it. We carry it. The next generation
   carries it forward. Every chapter answers: *what was carried, by whom, and
   what did it cost to keep?*
3. **The record does the arguing.** We publish sources and show where accounts
   differ. A brand that can be checked is more threatening to erasure than a
   brand that shouts, because it cannot be dismissed as decoration.

## 3. The generational spine

| Core story | Chapter | Object | Documented basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *They carried it.* | **001 — ROOTS** | Cloth | Majdalawi weaving survived the expulsion and kept the town's name |
| *We carry it.* | **002 — SABR** | Cactus | *Sabr* means patience; the cactus still marks depopulated village sites |
| *The next generation carries it forward.* | **003 — BALADI** | Seed | *Baladi* means "my country"; heirloom seeds saved and replanted across generations |

The spine was already latent in the brand's own core story. The research
supplied the objects; we did not reverse-engineer history to fit a slogan —
which is the failure mode this whole repository is built against.

## 4. What we say, and what we never say

**We say:** what happened, with sources · who carried it · what it cost ·
where the record is unresolved · what we do not know.

**We never say:**
- Any slogan that could be printed by a brand that did no research.
- A symbol's meaning that a source does not give it.
- "Resistance" as a decorative word on a garment.
- Anything that turns a living political situation into a finished heritage
  story. **Dispossession is ongoing.** The past tense is a choice and we do not
  make it carelessly.

## 5. The hardest question, kept open

Framing resistance as *continuity* carries a real risk: that it softens
dispossession into heritage, making a political crime feel like a folk
tradition. Heritage is comfortable. Dispossession is not supposed to be.

We do not consider this resolved. It is logged as **Question 6 in
`cultural-research/dossier-002-sumud-sabr-baladi.md`, blocking**, and it must
be put to a Palestinian reviewer in exactly those words. If the answer is that
our framing depoliticises the subject, the framing changes — not the research.

## 6. Political speech

MAJDAL is a brand, not a political organisation, and it should not pretend to a
mandate it does not have. But the subject is inherently political and neutrality
would be its own dishonesty.

**Position:** MAJDAL states documented history plainly, including the parts that
are uncomfortable — expulsion, erasure, a town renamed. It does not issue
positions on contemporary political questions in the founder's voice as though
the brand were a movement. When it speaks, it speaks about **what it can
evidence**: this town, this craft, this cloth, these people, this money.

Where we have a platform and something verifiable to amplify, we amplify
Palestinian voices rather than substituting for them — paid, credited, and on
their terms.

## 7. Test before publishing

1. Is every factual claim tiered and sourced?
2. Is this a noun with a history, or an adjective with a mood?
3. Could a brand that did no research have made this? → kill it.
4. Does it use suffering to sell? → kill it.
5. Does it treat an ongoing situation as finished history?
6. Would a Palestinian researcher recognise this as accurate, or as a brand
   borrowing their material?

Question 6 is the one that matters. Until Gate 2 exists, we cannot answer it
ourselves — which is precisely why no cultural claim reaches a garment first.
