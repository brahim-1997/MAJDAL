# MAJDAL — BRAND BOOK v0.1

مجدل — *"tower"*. A weaving town on the southern coast of Palestine.
Approximately 2,000 looms. 11,000 people. Taken in 1948.
The town left the map. The cloth kept its name: **Majdalawi**.

**ROOTED IN HISTORY. BUILT FOR TOMORROW.**

---

## 1. Why MAJDAL exists

Most Palestinian-inspired clothing is iconography applied to a blank garment.
The garment is incidental; it is a surface for a symbol.

MAJDAL inverts this. The town we are named after was a **textile town** — the
weaving centre of the whole Gaza District, where looms stood inside family
houses and a single eight-metre piece of cloth took a weaver one to two months.
When its people were expelled, they carried the craft with them and named it
after the place they had lost.

So MAJDAL is not a brand that puts Palestine on clothes.
**MAJDAL is a clothing brand descended from a clothing town.**

That distinction is the entire strategy. It decides the product (cloth quality
is the message), the naming (we use the weavers' own fabric names), the
community (THE ROOTS — people who carry something forward), and the reason a
person in Berlin or Manchester or Amman wears it (this is real, and it is good).

> They carried it.
> We carry it.
> The next generation carries it forward.

## 2. What we are and are not

| MAJDAL is | MAJDAL is not |
| --- | --- |
| A streetwear house with a craft lineage | A heritage or folk-costume brand |
| Heavyweight, oversized, raw, editorial | Souvenir or slogan clothing |
| Specific — named places, named fabrics, named people | Vaguely "inspired by the region" |
| Political by existing, precisely | A permanent protest placard |
| Scarce because we are small | Fake-scarce as a sales tactic |
| Transparent about money | Vague about "giving back" |

**The souvenir test.** Before any product: *would this exist in an airport gift
shop?* If yes, kill it. **The craft test:** *does this respect cloth?* If no, kill it.

## 3. Naming system — the weavers named it better

Every MAJDAL colourway takes its name from a **documented Majdalawi fabric**.
This is the brand's most distinctive asset and it costs nothing to own, because
it is true. (See `cultural-research/al-majdal-dossier.md` §3.)

| Colourway | From | Documented meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `IKHDARI` | إخضري | bands of red and green |
| `JILJILEH` | جلجلة | dark red bands |
| `MALAK` | ملك | silk |
| `JI'NNEH U NAR` | جنة ونار | "heaven and hell" |
| `NASHEQ ROHOH` | — | "breath of the soul" |
| `ABU MITAYN` | — | "father of two hundred" |

Three rules, no exceptions:

1. **Honesty of colour.** `IKHDARI` was red and green bands. A garment may
   carry the name only if its colours genuinely reference that description.
2. **Always teach.** Every product page states which fabric the name comes
   from and what it meant. The name is never decoration.
3. **Cleared first.** `JI'NNEH U NAR`, `NASHEQ ROHOH` and `ABU MITAYN` are
   **blocked pending P0 review** (`cultural-research/REVIEWS.md`). We do not
   assume a weaver's phrase is ours to sell.

## 4. The code: 48

48 means 1948, and it means عرب ٤٨ — the documented shorthand Palestinians
already use. For MAJDAL it marks **continuity**: the people who stayed, the
people who were driven out, the cloth that kept its name.

- Chapter 001 — ROOTS is produced in **480 pieces**, because that is the actual
  first production run our budget finances. The number is real. If the PO
  changes, the published number changes.
- 48 appears as a quiet mark — woven label, size tab, inner neck, box print.
- It is **never** explained as mystical or numerological. Asked what it means,
  the answer is one sentence: *1948 — and everyone who has carried this since.*

## 5. Voice

Short lines. Declarative. Specific nouns over adjectives. A loom is a loom.

Write: *"Eight metres of cloth. One to two months of work. One dress."*
Don't write: *"Inspired by the timeless traditions of a rich heritage."*

- Never perform grief. State the record and let it land.
- Never use the community as a marketing asset in front of the community.
- Arabic and Latin sit as equals — never Arabic as ornament behind English.
- No exclamation marks. No hype punctuation. No "drop soon 🔥".
- If a sentence could belong to any brand, delete it.

## 6. THE ROOTS

The community is not a mailing list; it is the brand's first objective and it
outranks revenue. Full mechanics in `strategy/COMMUNITY-THE-ROOTS.md`.

Membership is free and earned by participation, not purchase. Members get the
archive first, chapter access windows, and a real vote on one decision per
chapter. Nobody's position in THE ROOTS is improved by spending more money.

## 7. Impact — 15%

15% of every eligible product sale is committed to supporting people in
Palestine, published to the piece in a public ledger.

The system is built so we **cannot** overstate it: funds move through
`accrued → transferred → verified`, each shown separately, and the headline
number is the *verified* one. Before anything has been sold, the page says
zero. See `strategy/IMPACT-POLICY.md` and `impact/`.

## 8. Chapters

MAJDAL releases in chapters, not seasons. A chapter is a subject, researched
and documented, with garments as its artefacts. Chapter 001 — ROOTS is the
lineage itself: al-Majdal, the looms, the name that survived.

A chapter closes and does not return. No restocks of a closed chapter — but
core garments may continue as a permanent line under a different name, because
pretending a good hoodie is unrepeatable is fake scarcity.

## 9. Red lines

1. Never invent Palestinian history, symbolism or meaning.
2. Never copy another brand's logo, type, slogans, graphics, campaigns or
   distinctive identity — including ADISH, who have worked with Majdalawi cloth.
   Extract principles, build original MAJDAL implementations.
3. Never claim impact we cannot evidence.
4. Never manufacture scarcity.
5. Never make every post a sales post.
6. Never use images of Palestinian suffering to sell a garment.
7. Never let a cultural claim skip Gate 2.
