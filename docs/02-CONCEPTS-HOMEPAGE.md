# 02 — THREE HOMEPAGE CONCEPTS

Three genuinely different answers, then a recommendation with its costs.

---

## CONCEPT 1 — THE SIGNAL
*Cinematic cold open. The brief's §10, executed properly.*

Black. `48` in the corner, small. `MAJDAL / مجدل`. `THE ROOTS REMAIN.` A
fragment of the map resolves out of the dark. `ENTER THE ARCHIVE`. The world
opens beneath.

**Feels like:** arriving somewhere after hours. Maximum first-contact drama.

**For:** delivers "I discovered something" immediately; strong emotional hit;
highly shareable as a video clip; matches the brief literally.

**Against — and these are serious:**
- **A cold open is the single most imitated underground device on the web.** It
  reads as "underground" precisely because everyone reaching for underground
  uses it. It is a *signifier* of mystery rather than mystery itself.
- Punishes returning visitors, who are the audience that matters most.
- Content behind an animation is content a crawler and a screen reader meet last.
- It costs the user nothing to watch. **Mystery that costs nothing is decoration.**

**Verdict:** right instinct, wrong load-bearing role. Excellent as an *overture*.
Fatal as a *foundation*.

---

## CONCEPT 2 — THE ARCHIVE DESK
*The homepage is the catalogue. No hero at all.*

You land on a dense typographic register. Catalogue numbers, place names in
Arabic and Latin, districts, coordinates, evidence tiers, chapter references —
the live index of everything MAJDAL knows, most recent first, with gaps marked
`UNSOURCED` and contested records marked `CONTESTED`. The map is one *view* of
this same dataset. The chapter and its objects are records in it too.

**Feels like:** opening a working archive at night. Someone is mid-catalogue.

**For:**
- **A generic streetwear brand cannot copy this.** Copying it requires having an
  archive. Concepts 1 and 3 are visual moves clonable in a weekend; this is not.
- It puts MAJDAL's actual advantage — real research — on the front page.
- Fast, indexable, accessible, and it scales: the homepage improves as the
  archive grows, with no redesign.
- Visible incompleteness recruits contributors. The gaps are the call to action.
- Unmistakably not an ecommerce template. It is not even shaped like one.

**Against:**
- **Cold on first contact.** A register does not move you the way an image does.
- Demands real content immediately; thin, it looks like an empty database.
- Harder to route to product — commerce is genuinely secondary here.
- Typographically unforgiving: it only works if the typography is excellent.

---

## CONCEPT 3 — THE LAND FIRST
*The map is the homepage.*

Near-black screen. The land emerges as you move. Everything — chapter, archive,
objects, community — is reached by travelling the map. No conventional page.

**Feels like:** one enormous idea, committed to completely.

**For:** unforgettable; the purest expression of "the land remembers"; a single
strong idea that a brand can be known for.

**Against — and they compound:**
- **Every route in the site is gated behind a canvas.** Catastrophic for SEO: the
  archive's search value disappears.
- Heavy first load on mobile data, which is most of the audience.
- Touch has no hover, so the core interaction must be rebuilt for phones.
- Accessibility becomes a parallel build, not a property of the design.
- **Commerce becomes hostile.** A person who wants a hoodie must navigate a map.
- Highest build cost of the three, and the hardest to change later.

**Verdict:** the most exciting concept and the most dangerous. It optimises for
the first visit at the expense of every subsequent one.

---

## RECOMMENDATION

> **Build Concept 2 as the foundation. Use Concept 1 as a restrained overture.
> Put Concept 3 one click away, at `/map`, where it can be brilliant without
> holding the site hostage.**

**Why 2 and not 3.** Both deliver discovery. Only one survives contact with
reality — mobile data, screen readers, search engines, returning visitors, and a
person who arrives wanting to buy a hoodie. Concept 3 spends the entire
performance and accessibility budget on the first thirty seconds.

**Why 2 and not 1.** A cold open makes the *site* feel discovered. An archive
makes the *brand* feel discovered. The second is durable; the first wears off on
the second visit and becomes an obstacle on the tenth.

**The precedent.** This is not a theory. **Places+Faces began in 2013 as a place
to dump an archive of backstage photographs** — a Tumblr — and became zines, a
magazine, club nights and then product. The most culturally credible brand in
the UK underground set was built archive-first, product-second. See
`strategy/WEB-PATTERNS-UK-UNDERGROUND.md` §1.

**The deciding argument.** Apply the brief's own test — *could another generic
streetwear brand easily copy this?* Concept 1: yes, in a weekend. Concept 3:
yes, expensively, with any content. **Concept 2: not without doing the
research.** Only one of these three is protected by the work already done.

**The cost, stated honestly.** Concept 2 is colder on first contact, and that is
a real loss. Mitigations, all cheap:
1. **The overture** — first visit only, under 1.2s, skippable, never gating
   content, never shown again (stored locally, degrading safely to no overture).
2. **One emotional full-bleed moment** immediately below the register: the core
   story set large, at rest, with the chapter object beside it.
3. **The register is written, not dumped.** Each row carries one human line, not
   just metadata. A record can be cold; a sentence cannot.

**Composite homepage order:**

| # | Section | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Overture** (first visit) | 48 → MAJDAL/مجدل → THE ROOTS REMAIN |
| 1 | **The signal** | Wordmark, chapter, one fact, one way in |
| 2 | **The register** | Live archive index — the brand's real asset |
| 3 | **The land** | Map fragment, alive, linking to `/map` |
| 4 | **The story** | The three lines, at rest, full bleed |
| 5 | **The spine** | Cloth → cactus → seed |
| 6 | **The object** | Chapter 001's garments as records |
| 7 | **The roots** | Join; what do you carry? |
| 8 | **The ledger** | The 15%, honest, currently zero |
