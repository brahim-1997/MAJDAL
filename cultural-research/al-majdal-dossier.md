# DOSSIER 001 — AL-MAJDAL & MAJDALAWI WEAVING

**Status:** Gate 1 (internal research). **Gate 2 external review: NOT DONE.**
**Compiled:** 2026-09-21
**Why this dossier exists:** the brand is named MAJDAL. Everything the name
claims has to be defensible. This is also the single most important strategic
document in the repository, because it establishes that MAJDAL is a *textile*
brand with a *textile* ancestor — not a clothing brand borrowing a flag.

---

## 1. The finding that defines the brand

`VERIFIED` — **Al-Majdal was the primary textile and weaving centre of the
Gaza District.** During the late Ottoman and British Mandate periods it
developed into the region's dominant weaving town. While cotton production
declined elsewhere in Palestine during the 19th century, al-Majdal's weavers
sustained their industry by importing raw thread from Egypt, India and Europe.
By the 1940s approximately **2,000 looms** were operating in the town, very
often installed inside private homes rather than in workshops.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; Sunbula; Handmade Palestine; The Jerusalem Fund.*

**Strategic consequence.** MAJDAL does not have to invent a reason to make
clothes. The town the brand is named after made cloth, at scale, in its own
houses, for the whole south of Palestine. The brand's legitimacy comes from
continuing a *craft* lineage, not from decorating garments with iconography.

This is the line between MAJDAL and generic Palestine merchandise, and it is
the answer to "why does this brand exist?".

---

## 2. The place

`VERIFIED` — **Name.** *Majdal* (مجدل) is Arabic for "tower" / "fortress", a
place-name element found across the Levant, typically marking elevated or
fortified sites. Related places include Majdal Yaba, Majdal Bani Fadil,
Majdal Shams, and al-Majdal on the Sea of Galilee. Our al-Majdal is the
coastal one, distinguished as **al-Majdal Asqalan** (المجدل عسقلان), sited
inland of ancient Ascalon.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdal"; Wikipedia "Majdal Shams"; palquest.*

`VERIFIED` — **Population.** Approximately **11,000 residents** at 1948.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; palquest.*

`CONTESTED` — **Date and manner of depopulation.** Sources do not agree and we
must not flatten this.
- Several sources state the town's population was forcibly expelled in **1948**
  during the Nakba, and its textile industry destroyed.
- At least one source dates the depopulation to **1951**.
- The wider historical record describes al-Majdal being taken by Israeli
  forces in **late 1948**, with the remaining Palestinian population confined
  and then expelled **in stages**, the last transfers to the Gaza Strip
  completed around **1950**.

**How MAJDAL states this until Gate 2 review:** "Al-Majdal was taken in 1948.
Its remaining residents were expelled in stages over the following years, most
to Gaza." We do not print a single hard date on a garment until a historian
confirms it. **Open question for reviewer: the precise expulsion timeline.**

`VERIFIED` — **The site today.** The Israeli city of **Ashkelon** was founded
in 1949 at the Palestinian town of al-Majdal, approximately 4 km inland from
ancient Ascalon. The historic town site lies within present-day Ashkelon.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Ashkelon"; Wikipedia "Majdal".*

`CONTESTED / UNVERIFIED FOR PRINT` — **Coordinates.** Modern Ashkelon sits at
roughly **31.67° N, 34.57° E**. This is the *modern city*, not a surveyed
fix on the historic town centre. **Do not print coordinates on a garment on
this basis.** Requires verification against Mandate-era survey maps.
**Open question for reviewer.**

## 3. The craft

`VERIFIED` — **Who wove, and how.** Weaving in al-Majdal was traditionally
done by **men**, on **single-treadle looms**. Cloth was mostly cotton and
wool based, and characteristically **striped**.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; Sunbula; Al Rawiya.*

> Note for campaign copy: the "Palestinian embroidery is women's work" framing
> is accurate for *tatreez* but wrong for *Majdalawi weaving*, which was a male
> trade. Do not merge the two crafts into one story. They are different
> practices, different genders, different tools, different regions.

`VERIFIED` — **Thread and colour.** Documented practice combines **black and
indigo cotton** with **fuchsia and turquoise silk**, producing a bold striped
textile — durable, with a characteristic sheen.
*Sources: Handmade Palestine; Sunbula.*

**Strategic consequence.** The documented Majdalawi palette is *not* muted.
Black and indigo grounds cut with hot fuchsia and turquoise. This gives MAJDAL
a historically grounded accent pair that no competitor can claim, and it saves
the brand from the washed-neutral sameness of every other heavyweight label.
See `brand/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` — these are the ARCHIVE accents.

`VERIFIED` — **Scale of a piece.** Cloth was commonly sold in **eight-metre
pieces**, the quantity needed for a single thobe. A weaver took **one to two
months** to complete a piece.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; Sunbula.*

`VERIFIED` — **Named fabrics.** The three major fabrics produced were:

| Name | Documented meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **`malak`** (ملك) | silk |
| **`ikhdari`** (إخضري) | bands of red and green |
| **`jiljileh`** (جلجلة) | dark red bands |

These were used for **festival dresses throughout southern Palestine**.
Many other fabrics carried poetic names, including:

| Name | Documented translation |
| --- | --- |
| **`ji'nneh u nar`** (جنة ونار) | "heaven and hell" |
| **`nasheq rohoh`** | "breath of the soul" |
| **`abu mitayn`** | "father of two hundred" |

*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; Birzeit University Museum; Sunbula.*

**Strategic consequence — this is the brand's colourway system.** MAJDAL does
not need to invent product names. The weavers of al-Majdal already named their
cloth, and they named it better than any agency would: *heaven and hell*,
*breath of the soul*, *father of two hundred*. Every MAJDAL colourway is named
after a documented Majdalawi fabric, and every product page states which
fabric the name comes from and what it meant. The naming system *teaches* the
craft while it sells the garment.

Rule: a colourway may only use one of these names if the garment's actual
colours honestly reference that fabric's documented description. `ikhdari` is
red and green. It cannot be a grey hoodie.

## 4. After 1948

`VERIFIED` — **The craft survived displacement.** After al-Majdal was
depopulated, former residents **continued to weave in the style** after being
relocated to the Gaza Strip and elsewhere. The craft took its name from the
lost town: *Majdalawi*, "of al-Majdal".
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; The Jerusalem Fund; Nol Collective.*

**This is the brand's core story, and it is documented, not invented:**
the town was erased from the map and the cloth kept its name.

`VERIFIED` — **Decline.** Imported machine-made clothing flooded the market.
Handmade textiles were comparatively expensive, demand fell, and traditional
fabrics were displaced by modern garments.
*Sources: Handmade Palestine; Al Rawiya.*

`VERIFIED` — **Revival attempt.** In the **1990s** the Palestinian Authority
attempted to revitalise the tradition in the Gaza Strip.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Majdalawi weaving"; Sunbula.*

`CONTESTED` — **How many weavers remain.** Sources vary between "a handful of
weavers in the Gaza Strip", "his family is among the last still practising",
and "only one Palestinian artisan". We do **not** publish a number.
MAJDAL's wording: "a very small number of weavers still work in this
tradition; accounts of exactly how many differ."
**Open question for reviewer.** Note also that any figure predates or is
unreliable given conditions in Gaza; a number stated confidently would be
both unverifiable and careless.

## 5. Adjacent craft — tatreez (distinct practice, do not merge)

`VERIFIED` — Tatreez is Palestinian **cross-stitch embroidery**, traditionally
made and worn in rural areas. Specific patterns and colours historically
indicated the wearer's **regional origin, social standing and marital status** —
a visual language. On **15 December 2021**, at the 16th session of the
Intergovernmental Committee for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural
Heritage, UNESCO inscribed "the art of embroidery in Palestine" on the
Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
*Sources: UNESCO via The New Arab, Middle East Eye, Arab America; Wikipedia "Tatreez"; CNN.*

`CONTESTED` — Claims that tatreez is "3,000 years old" appear in popular
coverage. Embroidery in the region is certainly ancient; the continuity of
*this specific practice* over three millennia is not something we can source
rigorously. **MAJDAL does not repeat the 3,000-year claim.**

**Design rule.** MAJDAL uses **tatreez-inspired geometry** — original
constructions built on the cross-stitch grid. We do not copy a specific
regional motif and we do not assign meanings to motifs. If we ever want to use
a specific documented motif, that is a collaboration with a named embroiderer
and a Gate 2 review, not a design decision.

## 6. "48" — the core code

`VERIFIED` — Palestinians who remained inside the 1949 armistice line are
colloquially known as **"48 Arabs"** (عرب ٤٨, *ʿArab Thamāniya wa-ʾArbaʿīn*).
The "48" refers to 1948. In repeated surveys a majority self-identify as
Palestinian.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Arab citizens of Israel"; Wikipedia "Green Line (Israel)".*

**What 48 means for MAJDAL.** It is a documented shorthand already in use by
Palestinians for Palestinians. We use it as a marker of **continuity — the
people who remained, the people who left, the cloth that kept its name**.

**What 48 must never become.** We do not invent numerology for it. We do not
explain it as a mystical code. We do not use it as a puzzle with no referent.
When someone asks what 48 means, the answer is one plain sentence: *1948 — and
everyone who has carried this since.* If we cannot say it plainly, we are
misusing it.

## 7. Open questions for Gate 2 review

1. Precise expulsion timeline for al-Majdal (1948 / staged / completed 1950 / 1951).
2. Surveyed coordinates of the historic town centre, if printable at all.
3. Arabic orthography and transliteration of every fabric name above,
   especially `jiljileh` / `jaljali` and `ikhdari`, which appear in variant spellings.
4. Whether `ji'nneh u nar` carries any connotation that makes it inappropriate
   for a commercial colourway. **We must not assume it is safe to use.**
5. Whether any living weaver or descendant community should be consulted
   before the brand commercialises the term *Majdalawi* at all — and if so, who.
6. Current status and population of Majdalawi weaving practice.

**Question 4 and Question 5 are blocking.** No garment ships carrying a
Majdalawi fabric name until they are answered by a named reviewer.
