# DOSSIER 002 — SUMUD, SABR, BALADI
## Resistance as continuity: the land, the patience, the seed

**Status:** Gate 1 (internal research). **Gate 2 external review: NOT DONE.**
**Compiled:** 2026-09-21
**Why this dossier exists:** MAJDAL's subject is Palestinian resistance
expressed as *continuity* — each generation producing the next. That is a real,
documented tradition with its own vocabulary. This dossier establishes that
vocabulary so the brand never has to invent one.

**The danger this dossier is written against.** "Resistance" is the easiest
theme in the world to do badly. Done badly it becomes a slogan on a t-shirt,
which is the exact generic-Palestine-merchandise outcome the brand exists to
avoid. Done properly it is specific, documented, and concerned with *what
survived and who carried it*. Every entry below is chosen because it is a noun,
not an adjective — a plant, a seed, a key, a word with a history.

---

## 1. SUMUD (صمود) — steadfastness

`VERIFIED` — **Meaning.** *Sumud* is usually translated "steadfastness" or
"steadfast perseverance". It describes Palestinian everyday, largely nonviolent
resistance centred on **remaining present on the land**. Sources consistently
note it does not translate cleanly — neither "steadfastness" nor "resilience"
carries its full weight.
*Sources: palquest; Institute for Palestine Studies; Wikipedia "Sumud".*

`VERIFIED` — **History of the term.** Sumud entered political discourse as a
national symbol in the **1960s**, and became a formal strategic concept in the
**1970s–80s**, when the PLO used it to emphasise maintaining physical presence
on the land. In **1978** the PLO recommended *sumud* as a way of helping people
remain in Palestine. Its practice is traced back further, to resistance during
the **1920s and 1930s**.
*Sources: palquest; Institute for Palestine Studies; CESRAN; Journal of Holy Land and Palestine Studies.*

`VERIFIED` — **Not a fixed definition.** Scholarship describes sumud as a
*continuum* of goals and practices responsive to changing conditions, broadly
understood today as the actions people take against politics of erasure.
*Sources: Institute for Palestine Studies; PMC (Social ecology of resilience and Sumud).*

**What sumud gives MAJDAL.** The word for the brand's entire thesis. MAJDAL's
resistance is not confrontation imagery — it is *staying, continuing, carrying*.
The weavers of al-Majdal who kept weaving after expulsion are a textbook
instance of sumud, and we did not have to stretch a definition to get there.

**Rule.** We do not use *sumud* as a product name or a printed slogan until
Gate 2 clears it. It is a serious political-cultural term with a specific
history, not a word to put on a hoodie because it sounds good.

## 2. SABR (صبر) — the cactus, and patience

This is the strongest single find in this dossier.

`VERIFIED` — **The double meaning.** The prickly pear cactus is known
colloquially as *sabr* (صبر) — which is also the Arabic word for **patience**
/ endurance.
*Sources: Avery Review "صَبْر — Patience as Resistance"; Middle East Eye; Atlas Obscura.*

`VERIFIED` — **The cactus marks the erased villages.** Rows of cactus,
formerly planted as field boundaries and fences, **still stand at the sites of
depopulated Palestinian villages** and mark where they were. Sources describe
efforts to conceal the ruins of over 500 depopulated villages, and the cactus —
deeply rooted — persisting through them, showing traces of where those villages
stood.
*Sources: Avery Review; Middle East Eye; Dazed MENA (Al-Wah'at Collective).*

`VERIFIED` — **The proverb.** *saber as-sabbar* — "the patience of the cactus".
*Source: Avery Review.*

`CONTESTED` — **A shared and disputed symbol.** The same plant (*sabra* in
Hebrew) is also used as a symbol by Israelis, for native-born Israelis. The
plant therefore carries competing claims. **MAJDAL states the Palestinian
meaning we are working with, and does not pretend the symbol is uncontested.**
*Sources: Atlas Obscura; Times of Israel; Rogue Valley Times.*

**What sabr gives MAJDAL — and why it connects directly to Chapter 001.**
Al-Majdal is a depopulated village. The cactus is what stands at depopulated
villages. This is not a thematic link we constructed; it is the same landscape.
Chapter 001 is the cloth that kept the name. Chapter 002 is the plant that kept
the place.

Sabr is also the answer to the "how do we do resistance without slogans"
problem. It is patience, rootedness, and a refusal to be erased — expressed by
a plant, not a fist. It is visually distinctive, it is a real botanical form
that yields original geometry, and no UK streetwear brand owns it.

**Rule.** The cactus is drawn as MAJDAL's own original construction. We do not
reproduce another artist's cactus artwork, and we do not assign it meanings
beyond the documented ones above.

## 3. BALADI (بلدي) — the seed, and fecundity

This is the literal answer to "the land keeps producing new generations".

`VERIFIED` — **Meaning.** *Baladi* is the term for heirloom seeds and
translates directly as **"my country"**.
*Sources: The Jerusalem Fund, "Baladi, Palestinian Heirloom Seeds as a Subversive Archive" (2026); Palestine Heirloom Seed Library.*

`VERIFIED` — **The Palestine Heirloom Seed Library.** Founded in **2014** by
**Vivien Sansour**, a Palestinian writer, artist and agricultural
conservationist, to preserve and redistribute indigenous Palestinian seed
varieties. It is located in the village of **Battir**, a UNESCO World Heritage
site. It began when Sansour started asking her community for seeds to grow
*baladi bandora* — "my country's tomatoes" — a drought-resistant heirloom
tomato adapted to the region.
*Sources: Wikipedia "Vivien Sansour"; Organic Seed Alliance; Arab America; viviensansour.com.*

`VERIFIED` — **Named varieties in the collection** include *Abu Samara*
(wheat), *Bamyeh* (okra), *Bandura Baladiye* (tomato), *Bitinjan Battiri*
(eggplant), *Fakous* (Armenian cucumber), *Jadu'i* (watermelon), *Jarjeer*
(arugula), *Kousa* (courgette), *Molokhia*, *Sabanikh* (spinach), *Silq Baladi*
(chard), *Yakteen* (gourd).
*Sources: Palestine Heirloom Seed Library; Organic Seed Alliance.*

`VERIFIED` — **Generational meaning.** Baladi seeds reflect a land-based
knowledge developed over generations, from the westernmost arc of the Fertile
Crescent, carrying millennia of cultivation and adaptation. Stewardship efforts
include safeguarding varieties in diaspora with the stated hope of returning
them home.
*Sources: The Jerusalem Fund (2026); Organic Seed Alliance (2025).*

**What baladi gives MAJDAL.** *Fécondité* — the thing the founder asked for —
already exists in Palestinian practice as a named, living, documented project.
A seed is the most precise possible object for "each generation carries it
forward": it is literally the mechanism by which one generation hands the next
something that can grow. The word means "my country".

**Rule — and a hard constraint.** Any use of actual seeds in product or
activation is subject to **phytosanitary and customs law**; seeds frequently
cannot be posted across borders. See `strategy/UK-UNDERGROUND-PLAYBOOK.md` §5,
where this is treated as a design constraint rather than discovered late.
Any association with the Palestine Heirloom Seed Library requires **their prior
written agreement and payment** — we do not borrow another project's name.

## 4. THE OLIVE TREE — inheritance

`VERIFIED` — **Passed between generations.** It is customary for families to
pass down olive trees to children and grandchildren, entrusting future
generations with their care. Olives and leaves are commonly read as hope and
heritage; roots and uprooting as displacement.
*Sources: Middle East Eye; Days of Palestine; Wikipedia "List of national symbols of Palestine".*

`CONTESTED` — **Age claims.** Popular coverage states Palestine holds some of
the world's oldest olive trees, "some tracing back almost 5,000 years".
Individual tree ages of this order are difficult to verify. **MAJDAL does not
repeat a specific number.**

**Design rule.** The olive is the single most predictable symbol available to
this brand. `brand/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` already bans an olive-tree logo. The olive
enters MAJDAL as **colour** (deep olive, already in the palette) and as the
*inheritance* idea — not as a drawn tree.

## 5. THE KEY (مفتاح, miftah) — return

`VERIFIED` — During the Nakba an estimated **750,000 Palestinians** were
displaced, and many took their house keys with them, expecting to return. The
key has become a representation of the right of return.
*Sources: Middle East Eye; Wikipedia "List of national symbols of Palestine"; KUVRD.*

**Use rule — deliberately restrictive.** The key is among the most widely
reproduced Palestinian symbols, including on mass-market merchandise. Putting a
key graphic on a hoodie is the souvenir test failing in real time.

MAJDAL's position: the key is **referenced in the archive, not printed on
product.** If it ever appears, it appears as a documented object — a photograph
of a specific family's key, with that family's consent and their story — never
as a decorative icon.

## 6. THE GENERATIONAL SPINE

Placed together, the material resolves into the brand's chapter structure, and
it maps exactly onto the core story that already existed:

| Core story line | Chapter | Object | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *They carried it.* | **001 — ROOTS** | The cloth | A craft that survived expulsion and kept the town's name |
| *We carry it.* | **002 — SABR** | The cactus | The land holding the record of what was erased |
| *The next generation carries it forward.* | **003 — BALADI** | The seed | Fecundity — what one generation saves, the next replants |

Cloth, plant, seed. Each is a documented noun with a verifiable history. None
is a slogan. This is what resistance looks like when it is held as continuity
rather than confrontation, and it is the answer to how MAJDAL can be
unmistakably political without becoming a placard.

## 7. Open questions for Gate 2 review

1. **Is it appropriate for a commercial streetwear brand to use *sumud*?** It
   is a term with a specific political history. **Blocking for any printed use.**
2. **Is *sabr*/the cactus appropriate as a chapter name and garment motif**,
   given it is a symbol claimed by more than one group? How should MAJDAL
   acknowledge that contest without flattening it?
3. **Is *baladi* appropriate as a chapter name**, and must the Palestine
   Heirloom Seed Library be consulted before we use the seed concept at all —
   even without naming them? **Assume yes until told otherwise.**
4. Transliteration and orthography of صمود, صبر, بلدي for any printed use.
5. Is MAJDAL's decision to keep the key *out* of product correct, or does it
   read as avoidance of the right of return? A Palestinian reviewer should
   decide this, not us.
6. Does framing resistance as *continuity* risk depoliticising the subject —
   softening dispossession into heritage? **This is the most important question
   in this dossier and it should be put to a reviewer bluntly.**

**Questions 1, 2, 3 and 6 are blocking.** Chapters 002 and 003 do not go into
production until a named Palestinian reviewer has answered them.
